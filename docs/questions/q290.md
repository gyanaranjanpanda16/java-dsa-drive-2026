# Q290 — Minimize the cost of connecting ropes

**Topic:** Heaps and greedy · **Level:** Medium

## Question and contract

Minimize the cost of connecting ropes. Implement the operation exposed by `long solve(int[] lengths)`. Nonnegative lengths; exact intermediate sums must fit long.

## Runnable example

```java
solve(new int[]{4,3,2,6})
```

Expected output:

```text

```

## How and why the answer works

A rope contributes its length once for every ancestor merge. In an optimal binary merge tree, two smallest weights can be siblings at greatest depth; exchanging larger leaves with them cannot increase cost. Contracting those siblings reduces to the same problem, justifying repeated smallest-pair merging.

## Walkthrough

For ropes [4,3,2,6], merge the two smallest 2 and 3 for cost 5 and put 5 back. Merge 4 and 5 for cost 9, then 6 and 9 for cost 15. The total cost is 5+9+15=29. Reinserting each merged length matters because it participates in later costs; greedily minimizing repeated contribution avoids expensive large ropes early.

## Complexity and tradeoffs

O(n log(n+1)) time; O(n) heap space. This cost describes the supplied representation and stated input contract. A more general version may need extra validation or a different representation; do not silently extend the assumptions.

- `PriorityQueue<Long> heap=new PriorityQueue<>();` — Repeatedly expose the two shortest remaining ropes.
- `for(int length:lengths) {` — Validate and widen lengths before any sums are formed.
- `if(length<0) throw new IllegalArgumentException("negative length");` — Physical rope lengths cannot be negative.
- `heap.offer((long)length);` — Preserve exact integer lengths in long arithmetic.
- `long cost=0;` — No join has been performed initially.
- `while(heap.size()>1) {` — One remaining rope means all joins are finished.
- `heap.offer(joined);` — The new rope participates in future joins.
- `return cost;` — Empty and singleton inputs require no joins.
