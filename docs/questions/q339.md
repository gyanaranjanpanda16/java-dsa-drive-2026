## How and why the answer works

For a non-root vertex, a child with low[child]>=entered[v] cannot reconnect above v after v is removed. A DFS root has no ancestor, so it is a cut vertex exactly when it has at least two DFS children.

## Walkthrough

For edges 0–1,1–2,1–3, removing vertex 1 separates vertices 0,2,3, so 1 is an articulation point. Removing a leaf does not disconnect the remaining graph. The DFS rule for a root differs from an internal vertex: multiple independent root child subtrees are the relevant condition.

## Complexity and tradeoffs

## Checks and edge cases

- Central vertex separates branches.
- Cycle reconnects around every removed vertex.
- Removing an endpoint does not increase component count.
